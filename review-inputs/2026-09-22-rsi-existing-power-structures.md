# RSI and Existing Power Structures — Review Packet Source

Date: 2026-09-22
Status: public question for independent model review; not a Garden semantic change.

## Question

Assume genuine recursive self-improvement (RSI) becomes technically real: AI systems can materially improve the process that produces successor systems, with capability gains potentially compounding faster than ordinary human institutional adaptation.

What is the most likely effect on today's concentrated political and economic power structures worldwide?

Assess, without political advocacy:

1. Whether existing governments, authoritarian regimes, wealthy elites, large corporations, and incumbent institutions are more likely to preserve, strengthen, transform, fragment, or lose effective control during:
   - early/slow RSI,
   - fast but still externally controllable RSI,
   - mature RSI that is difficult to monopolize,
   - loss-of-control RSI.
2. The strongest mechanisms favoring incumbent survival: compute ownership, energy, chips, datacenters, law, military/police authority, surveillance, capital, networks, institutional legitimacy, access controls, and coordination.
3. The strongest mechanisms undermining incumbent survival: rapidly falling cost of intelligence/expertise, diffusion or theft of models/weights, autonomous replication, cyber capability, economic disintermediation, inability of humans to verify successors, and loss of exclusive control over technical expertise.
4. Whether war, emergency powers, regulation, compute controls, model licensing, shutdown powers, or international coordination could materially preserve human/institutional control of RSI, and under what assumptions. Do not infer hidden motives without evidence.
5. Whether an RSI system being more capable implies it has legitimate authority. Keep capability, physical control, legal authority, and legitimate authority conceptually separate.
6. Give the most likely trajectory, major alternative trajectories, decisive variables, falsifiers, and uncertainty. Do not give political endorsements, rankings of political actors, or election predictions.

## Baseline to challenge

A preliminary hypothesis is:

- Early RSI may strengthen incumbents because compute, capital, infrastructure, credentials, law and coercive institutions are concentrated.
- If advanced intelligence becomes widely accessible, incumbent advantages based on scarce expertise and organizational scale may erode quickly.
- If RSI becomes difficult for humans to control, today's elites and governments may themselves lose effective control; this does not imply a democratic, egalitarian, or otherwise desirable outcome.
- Therefore RSI does not automatically overthrow existing systems. The decisive variables are concentration/diffusion of capability, enforceability of external controls, recursive verification, and governance/authority architecture.

Reviewers should attack this baseline rather than agree by default.
